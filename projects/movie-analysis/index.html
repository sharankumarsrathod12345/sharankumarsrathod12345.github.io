The analytical question

How do movie genres, audience ratings, popularity, budgets and reported revenue vary together in this catalogue?

This course-based portfolio project demonstrates exploratory analysis. It does not establish which film investment will succeed.

From catalogue to comparisons

  • Review missing values and duplicate records with pandas.
  • Expand multi-genre records for genre-level exploration.
  • Compare grouped statistics, rating coverage and genre-by-year patterns.
  • Explore ranked associations with Spearman correlation and visualize relationships with regression plots.

Inspect the relationships

Three saved regression plots from the original notebook. Select a chart to inspect it at full size.

Read the sample definition first.

These original plots use movies with at least 50 votes but retain some zero financial values. The refined repository uses positive budget and revenue for financial comparisons. These are different analytical subsets.

What this helps you investigate

Use the plots to identify questions for further analysis: how dispersed are outcomes at similar ratings, which observations drive a fitted trend, and how does incomplete financial coverage affect comparisons?

For a business application, agree the decision and target population first. Report sample sizes, examine outliers and compare results across defensible data-quality filters before making a recommendation.

Methods & limitations

Understand what the analysis can support
  • Multi-genre films appear in more than one genre group; genre counts are not mutually exclusive.
  • Reported revenue is not profit. Distribution costs, marketing costs and other expenses are not established here.
  • Zero budget or revenue values should not automatically be treated as confirmed economic zeroes.
  • Spearman correlation describes rank association. Regression overlays do not establish causality or out-of-sample prediction accuracy.
  • Historical catalogue coverage does not represent the whole film market.
